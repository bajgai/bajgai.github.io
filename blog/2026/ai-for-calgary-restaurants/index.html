Catering inquiries, menus and staff schedules: AI for Calgary restaurants

October 17, 2026

2026   ·   small-business   automation   calgary   restaurants     ·   local  

Running a restaurant is a lot of small jobs done fast, all day, with your hands full. Most of the admin happens late at night or before open: answering catering emails, updating the menu in four places, sorting out who can cover Saturday.

AI won’t cook or serve. But it’s good at that layer of admin, and in a business where margins are thin, every hour back matters.

Here’s where I’d start for a restaurant, cafe or food truck in Calgary.

1. Catering and group booking inquiries

Catering inquiries are high-value and slow to answer. Each one needs the same details (date, headcount, budget, dietary needs, pickup or delivery) and usually a few emails back and forth before you can quote.

What helps:

  • A short catering form on your website that asks for all of that up front.
  • An automatic reply that thanks them and confirms what happens next.
  • AI that reads the request and drafts a quote from your catering menu and pricing, for you to check and send.
  • A follow-up if they haven’t confirmed a few days later.

Calgary has busy catering seasons: Stampede in July, holiday parties in December, and graduation and wedding season in spring and summer. Those are the weeks a fast, complete first reply wins the booking.

2. Keeping the menu the same everywhere

Your menu probably lives in five places: your website, Google Business Profile, delivery apps, social media and a printed copy. Change a price or sell out of a dish and four of them are wrong.

What helps: keep one master menu (a spreadsheet works fine) and have changes flow out from there to the places that support it. Where a platform can’t be updated automatically, the automation at least produces a checklist of what to update by hand.

AI can also help write menu descriptions, translate the menu for customers who read another language, and flag likely allergens for you to double-check. Allergen information must always be confirmed by a person, never left to AI.

3. Answering the same questions

“Are you open on the holiday Monday?” “Do you have gluten-free options?” “Can I bring a cake?” “Is there parking?”

What helps: a simple FAQ that an AI assistant can use to answer messages on your website, with anything it isn’t sure about passed to you. Keep the FAQ short and current. When the answer changes, change it in one place.

4. Staff schedules and shift swaps

Scheduling tools like 7shifts, Homebase and Deputy already handle a lot of this, and if you’re still doing schedules in a group chat, moving to one of them is the first step.

What helps on top: automations that remind staff of shifts, collect availability in one place, and notify you only when a swap needs approval. AI can draft a first version of next week’s schedule from availability and expected busy times, which you then adjust.

5. Reviews

Restaurants get more reviews than most businesses, and replies matter. Use AI to draft replies in your voice, and reply to negative ones yourself. I wrote a whole post on replying to Google reviews in your own voice.

What to leave alone

  • Allergen and food safety decisions. A person checks every time.
  • Refunds and complaints about illness. Take these offline and handle them personally.
  • Your social media voice. AI can help with drafts and scheduling, but regulars can tell when a restaurant’s posts stop sounding like the people who run it.

Where to start

If catering is part of your business, start there. It’s the highest-value inquiry you get and the one most often left waiting. If not, start with the FAQ answers, because they save time every single day.
