Stop chasing quotes and invoices by hand

Niranjan Bajgai October 17, 2026 · 5 min read

Every small business has two piles of unfinished money: quotes that never got a yes or no, and invoices that never got paid. Both usually need the same thing, a polite nudge, and both get skipped because sending it feels awkward and there’s always something more urgent.

Automating the nudge takes the awkwardness out. It goes out on time, every time, in a friendly tone, and you only step in when someone replies.

Part 1: Quotes

Most quotes that go unanswered aren’t a no. The customer got busy, forgot, or has a question they didn’t ask. A short follow-up brings a lot of them back.

A follow-up schedule that isn’t pushy

  • Day 2 or 3: a check-in that offers help.
  • Day 7: a short reminder, with an easy way to say no.
  • Day 14: a final note that closes the loop.

Then stop. If someone replies at any point, the automation stops and the conversation comes to you.

Templates you can copy

Day 3

Hi Jordan, just checking you got the quote for the fence repair. Happy to answer any questions or adjust anything. If it’s easier, reply with a good time and I’ll give you a call.

Day 7

Hi Jordan, following up on the fence repair quote. If the timing or the price doesn’t work, no problem, just let me know and I’ll close it off. If you’d like to go ahead, reply here and we’ll get you booked in.

Day 14

Hi Jordan, I’ll close off the fence repair quote for now so it doesn’t clutter your inbox. If you want to pick it up again later, just reply to this email.

The “it’s fine to say no” line matters. It gets you answers, and an answer, even a no, is worth more than silence. Over time those answers tell you why quotes are lost.

Make the quotes themselves faster

Follow-ups only help if the quote went out quickly in the first place. If writing quotes is the slow part, a good template saves the most time. Part 2: Invoices

Start with what you already have

QuickBooks, Wave, FreshBooks, Xero and Square all have some form of automatic payment reminder. Check your settings today and turn it on. It’s free and takes five minutes.

What those built-in reminders usually can’t do is change the tone as an invoice gets older, skip a customer you’ve already spoken to, or tell you which invoices need a phone call. That’s where a small custom automation helps.

A reminder schedule

  • 3 days before due: a friendly heads-up with the payment link.
  • Due date: a short “this is due today”.
  • 7 days overdue: a polite reminder, asking if anything’s wrong with the invoice.
  • 21 days overdue: a firmer note, and a flag to you to pick up the phone.

Templates

7 days overdue

Hi Priya, a quick reminder that invoice 1042 for $640 was due last week. Here’s the payment link: [link]. If anything on the invoice doesn’t look right, just reply and I’ll sort it out.

21 days overdue

Hi Priya, invoice 1042 for $640 is now three weeks overdue. Could you let me know when I can expect payment? If there’s a problem, I’d rather hear about it so we can work it out.

Always include the payment link. The easier it is to pay, the faster people pay.

Where AI fits

AI isn’t needed for the reminders themselves. Where it helps is reading the replies. When a customer writes back “we’re waiting on our boss to approve it” or “can we split it into two payments?”, AI can sort the reply, pause the reminders, and send you a one-line summary so you know exactly what’s going on.
