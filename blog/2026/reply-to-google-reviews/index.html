Replying to Google reviews in your own voice, without writing every one

Niranjan Bajgai October 17, 2026 · 5 min read

Google reviews are the first thing a lot of customers read about you, and your replies are part of that. A business that answers every review looks open and paying attention. A business with forty unanswered reviews looks like nobody’s home.

The problem is volume. Writing a fresh, thoughtful reply to every “great service, thanks!” gets old fast, so most owners either stop replying or paste the same line under every review. Customers notice both.

Here’s how to use AI to draft replies that still sound like you, and where to draw the line.

What the difference looks like

Here’s a made-up review for a made-up hair salon, with the kind of reply most owners paste in and the kind this approach produces.

Review (5 stars): “Booked last minute for my sister’s wedding and Mei fixed the colour disaster I walked in with. Will be back!”

Generic reply: “Thank you for your review! We appreciate your business.”

AI draft in the owner’s voice: “So glad Mei could rescue the colour in time for the wedding! Hope the day was wonderful. See you next time. – Lena”

The AI draft takes no longer to approve than the generic line takes to paste, and it tells future customers that a real person reads what they write.

The rule: AI drafts, you decide

AI is good at the first draft. It’s fast, it doesn’t get bored, and it can pick out the detail in a review that’s worth mentioning. It is not good at judgement: whether to apologise, whether to take something offline, whether a review is fake.

So split the work:

  • Positive reviews (4 and 5 stars): AI drafts, you glance at it, it goes out.
  • Mixed reviews (3 stars): AI drafts, you edit before posting.
  • Negative reviews (1 and 2 stars): AI can suggest a calm starting point, but you write the reply yourself or rewrite the draft completely. Never auto-post these.

Step 1: Teach it your voice

Find five or six review replies you’ve written that you’re happy with. If you don’t have any, write them now, for a mix of positive and mixed reviews. These are your examples.

Then write down a few rules about how you talk to customers. For example:

  • Use the customer’s first name if they gave it.
  • Mention one specific thing from their review.
  • Keep it to two or three sentences.
  • Sign off with your first name, not “The Management”.
  • Never offer discounts or refunds in a public reply.

Step 2: Draft replies with any AI chat tool

You can do this today with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or similar. Paste in your examples and rules once, then paste a new review and ask for a reply in the same style.

A prompt that works:

Here are examples of how I reply to Google reviews for my business, and my rules. Write a reply to the new review below in the same voice. Mention one specific thing the customer said. Two or three sentences.

Read the draft out loud. If it sounds like something you’d never say, tell it why and ask again. After a few rounds you’ll have a set of examples that produce good drafts most of the time.

Step 3: Make it automatic (optional)

Copy and paste works fine for a few reviews a week. If you get more than that, or you have several locations, it’s worth automating:

  1. A new review arrives on your Google Business Profile.
  2. The automation sends it, along with your examples and rules, to an AI model.
  3. The draft lands somewhere you already look: your email, a Slack channel, or a simple approval list.
  4. You tap approve (or edit) and the reply is posted.

Negative reviews skip the queue and come straight to you with a notification, so you can reply quickly and carefully.

What not to automate

  • Anything that admits fault or makes promises. That’s a business decision, not a writing task.
  • Reviews that might be fake or from a competitor. Flag them to Google instead of replying.
  • Anything involving health, legal or personal details. If a review mentions a medical visit, a legal matter or a private situation, reply in general terms or take it offline. Don’t confirm someone was your client.

Why it’s worth doing

Replying consistently shows future customers how you treat people when things go right and when they don’t. It takes most owners a few minutes a day once the drafts are good, instead of an hour every few weeks of catching up.
