If you run a service business in Calgary, you already know the pattern. The phone rings when you're on a roof, under a sink, with a client, or driving down Deerfoot. You can't pick up. By the time you call back, they've booked someone else.

That missed call doesn't show up on any report. It's not an expense you can see. It's just a job that never happened. For most service businesses it's the biggest leak in the whole operation, and it's one of the cheapest to fix.

## Why it's worse here than it sounds

Calgary demand comes in waves. A hailstorm sends half the city looking for roofers, auto body shops and window repair in the same week. The first cold snap does the same for furnace and plumbing calls. Spring brings landscaping, decks and fences all at once.

Those are exactly the weeks you're too busy to answer the phone, and exactly the weeks customers call three businesses and go with whoever replies first. Speed wins the job more often than price does.

Those example figures are assumptions, not data from a real business. Even if you halve every one of them, most service businesses are still losing more each month than it costs to fix the problem once.

To find your real first number, check your phone's call log for one week and count every call you didn't answer and didn't call back within the hour.

## The fix: answer every caller in under a minute, automatically

The goal isn't to answer every call yourself. It's that nobody who reaches out is left hearing nothing.

A typical setup looks like this:

1. **Missed call, instant text.** When you can't pick up, the caller gets a text within a minute: "Sorry I missed you, I'm on a job. What can I help with? You can also book a time here." It comes from your business number, so it feels like you.
2. **AI sorts the reply.** When they text back, an AI model reads it and works out what it is: an emergency, a quote request, a booking, or a question it can answer from your own FAQ (hours, areas you cover, rough prices).
3. **The right next step goes out.** Bookings get your calendar link. Quote requests get your two or three standard questions (photos, address, timeline). Emergencies get flagged to your phone straight away.
4. **You get a summary, not a mess.** Instead of ten voicemails, you see a short list: who called, what they need, what's already been sent, and who's waiting on you.

The same flow works for website forms, Facebook and Instagram messages, and Google Business Profile chats, so every channel gets the same fast reply.
